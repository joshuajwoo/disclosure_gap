# Consent draft

Status: **approved for synthetic/developer testing only; not approved for recruitment**. Version 0.2.0, 2026-10-04. “Internal testing” means developers using fictional responses, not a usability study that records data from recruited people. Institutional contact, jurisdiction-specific language, compensation, retention duration, and review information must be completed by the responsible institution before real use.

## Study title and invitation

You are invited to take part in a research study about decisions to discuss personal concerns with trusted people and how those decisions relate to social experiences. You may participate only if you are 18–22 years old. Taking part is voluntary.

## What participation involves

If you agree, you will complete a baseline questionnaire, one short check-in each week for four weeks, and a follow-up questionnaire. The study asks whether you wanted to discuss a personal concern, the type of trusted person you had in mind, whether you talked with them, and structured questions about comfort and expected support. It does not ask for names, contact details, account access, exact locations, or the content of the concern. Estimated completion times must be inserted after usability testing.

## Risks or discomforts

Questions about social anxiety, support, or sharing may feel personal or uncomfortable. You may choose “Prefer not to answer” for optional questions or stop at any time. The application is not a crisis service and responses are not monitored to provide clinical help. The approved consent must include institutionally reviewed support information.

Privacy is a risk whenever data is collected online. The study reduces this risk by avoiding direct identifiers and free text, using a random participant ID, restricting access, and reporting aggregate results. Complete security cannot be guaranteed.

## Benefits and alternatives

You may not receive a direct benefit. The study may help researchers understand how to measure sharing decisions more carefully. The alternative is not to participate; choosing not to participate has no penalty. Personal trend summaries are descriptive and are not a diagnosis or recommendation.

## Data and confidentiality

The study stores structured answers under a random identifier. It uses an opaque session and recovery code rather than requesting contact information; only a hash of the recovery code is stored. Authorized researchers may receive a de-identified export. Results will be reported in aggregate, and small groups will not be published. The approved version must state hosting location, authorized roles, retention period, backup/deletion limits, any legal confidentiality limits, and whether de-identified data may be reused.

## Voluntary participation, withdrawal, and deletion

Participation is voluntary. You may stop without penalty. The application provides a withdrawal/deletion action. The approved policy must say what is deleted, timing, whether backup copies age out, and whether already aggregated results can be removed. Losing the recovery code may make it impossible to locate the record without collecting identity information.

## Compensation, costs, and contacts

The approved version must state compensation and costs, if any; a study-team contact; an independent participant-rights/IRB contact; and the protocol/review identifier.

## Consent statement

“I have read this information, had an opportunity to ask questions using the contact above, am 18–22 years old, and voluntarily agree to participate.” Options: `I agree`; `I do not agree`.

Record document version, agreement action, and timestamp. Provide the participant a durable copy of the consent information without exposing their study answers.
