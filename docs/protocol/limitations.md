# Interpretation and limitations

Lower disclosure is not inherently harmful. It may reflect healthy boundaries, cultural norms, privacy preference, lack of a safe relationship, concern about consequences, or the absence of a suitable opportunity. The primary feature is a behavioral self-report, not a symptom score, and the application must not score a person negatively for keeping something private.

The observational design can estimate association but cannot establish that an intention–action gap causes later social anxiety. Reverse causation, stable individual differences, time-varying stressors, perceived relationship safety, and measurement overlap can confound results. Baseline adjustment does not remove all confounding.

Weekly retrospective reports are subject to recall and interpretation differences. Four check-ins provide limited within-person information; missingness and attrition may be informative. The gap is undefined for someone who never reports wanting to share, and sparse audience observations make relationship-specific features unstable.

The APA SAD-D Adult severity measure is not a diagnosis. Although adult clinical and community studies support its psychometric properties and the official instructions support repeated use, evidence is not specific to a four-week U.S. convenience sample ages 18–22. The measure may provide more information at higher symptom severity and may have limited discrimination at the low end. Permission to reproduce an instrument and scientific suitability remain separate questions.

A convenience sample will not represent all people ages 18–22. Small samples limit precision, model complexity, subgroup analysis, and transportability. Subgroup results must be suppressed when disclosure risk is material. Ages 14–17 are outside scope and would require a separate protocol and protections.

Predictors about comfort and judgment may overlap conceptually with social-anxiety items. The primary behavioral feature, separate outcome form, preregistration, and leakage sensitivity analysis reduce but do not eliminate circularity. Null or negative results are informative and must be reported without reframing exploratory patterns as confirmation.
