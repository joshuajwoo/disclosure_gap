# Human-subjects review requirements

Status: **real recruitment prohibited pending formal determination/approval**.

## Determination

The proposed activity prospectively interacts with living people through surveys, collects private mental-health-related responses for a systematic investigation, and intends to contribute to generalizable knowledge. It should be treated as human-subjects research, not as internal product analytics or quality improvement.

Under the U.S. Common Rule, applicability depends on funding, institutional assurances/policies, and jurisdiction. Some survey research may qualify for an exemption, but investigators must not self-declare exemption where the responsible institution requires an IRB or human-research-protection-office determination. The NIH decision tool expressly says it is not the sole determination of exemption: https://www.grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/human-subjects/hs-decision

## Required next action

Before any real recruitment, the responsible investigator must submit the protocol to the relevant institution’s IRB or human research protection office and obtain a written determination (exempt, expedited/other review, not human-subjects research, or other locally applicable status). If there is no affiliated institution, obtain advice from a qualified independent IRB and legal/privacy counsel for the actual locations and recruitment method.

Submission materials should include the preregistration, recruitment text, consent, all questions, the instrument decision and reproduction terms, data flow, threat model, retention/deletion policy, compensation, participant resources, and analysis/disclosure plan. Recruitment, pilot feedback from real people, and collection must wait for the written determination and any required approval.

## Additional checks

- Confirm state/country privacy and research rules for participant and server locations.
- Confirm whether collecting mental-health questionnaire responses creates additional institutional or contractual obligations even without names.
- Confirm accessibility, language, compensation, and recruitment-channel requirements.
- Adults only: ages 14–17 require a separate protocol addressing parental permission/assent and additional protections.
- Record protocol number, reviewing body, determination, dates, approved versions, expiration/continuing-review terms, and reportable-event process.
